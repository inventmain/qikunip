I. Fundamentals

What Are the Differences Between Invention, Utility Model, and Design Patents?

Invention Patent: Protects novel technical solutions for products, processes, or improvements thereof. Represents the highest technical threshold, undergoes substantive examination, and provides a 20-year term from the filing date.

Utility Model Patent: Protects novel technical solutions relating to the shape, structure, or combination thereof of a product. Often termed "petty patents," they undergo preliminary examination without substantive review, grant quickly, and offer a 10-year protection term.

Design Patent: Protects aesthetic designs created for product shape, pattern, color, or combinations thereof that are fit for industrial application. Undergoes preliminary examination only, with a 15-year protection term.

How Long Does It Take to Grant an Invention Patent in China?

Under regular examination, the average pendency for Chinese invention patents is approximately 22 months. Timelines vary based on technical field complexity, examiner caseload, and drafting quality.

QiKun routinely leverages accelerated prosecution pathways—such as CNIPA Priority Examination, which can compress the grant cycle to 6–12 months for eligible core technologies.

What Is "High-Value Patent Layout"?

The fundamental difference from standard filing lies in the ultimate objective. Conventional filing aims merely to obtain a certificate; high-value patent layout aims to engineer commercial assets.

II. Procedures & Strategy

What Is the Step-by-Step Patent Application Lifecycle in China?

Technical disclosure → Patentability & prior art search → High-quality application drafting (claims and specifications) → Official filing → CNIPA acceptance & docketing → Preliminary examination → Publication & Substantive Examination (for inventions) → Notice of Allowance or Final Rejection.

Throughout this prosecution lifecycle, drafting rigor and Office Action response strategy determine commercial claim value. QiKun’s bench of former CNIPA examiners and trial litigators defends your scope at every milestone.

What Are the Costs Associated with Patent Filing?

Costs comprise two primary components:

  • Official CNIPA Fees: Statutory fees levied by CNIPA. For invention patents, initial filing and substantive examination fees total approximately RMB 3,450. Eligible micro-enterprises and individual applicants may qualify for a 70% to 85% statutory fee reduction.
  • Professional Agency Fees: Retainers for patent attorney drafting and prosecution. Pricing reflects expert allocation, technical depth, and claim quality. QiKun focuses on high-value prosecution, reflecting our commitment to enforceable commercial assets.

What Should I Do If My Patent Application Is Rejected by CNIPA?

A Final Rejection is by no means the end of the road. Applicants have a statutory 3-month window from receipt of the decision to file a Petition for Reexamination with the CNIPA Reexamination and Invalidation Department.

QiKun’s team possesses extensive experience in CNIPA appeal proceedings. We dissect examiner citations, pinpoint technical misunderstandings, craft persuasive legal briefs, and substantially elevate reexamination reversal rates.

How Do You Draft Robust, High-Value Patent Claims?

The claim set is the engine of the patent, defining the exact legal perimeter of exclusive rights. High-value claim drafting demands:

  • Multi-Tiered Claim Architecture: Broad independent claims establishing fundamental protection, backed by progressive dependent claims forming nested defensive fallbacks.
  • Judicial-Grade Term Precision: Exact lexicography crafted to prevent prosecution history estoppel while maximizing litigation resilience.
  • Predictive Competitive Blocking: Engineering claim language that anticipates and pre-empts competitor workarounds and substitute embodiments.
This technical foresight distinguishes master patent attorneys from volume-based typists, and defines QiKun's institutional standard.

Understanding Priority Rights

Priority right is an established international patent doctrine under the Paris Convention. It entitles an applicant, within 12 months for inventions and utility models (or 6 months for designs) from an initial filing, to claim the initial filing date for subsequent applications on the same subject matter.

Its core benefit: securing a vital window to refine engineering disclosures or execute PCT/foreign filings without intervening prior art compromising patent novelty.
