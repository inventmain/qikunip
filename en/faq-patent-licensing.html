I. Core Concepts

How Is a Patent's Monetization Value Evaluated?

Monetary patent valuation is a multi-dimensional analysis that cannot be reduced to simple formulas. QiKun’s valuation methodology synthesizes three core pillars:

  1. Legal Robustness: Breadth and precision of granted claims, vulnerability to prior art invalidation challenges, and remaining patent term.
  2. Technical Primacy: Whether the invention resolves decisive industry bottlenecks, its degree of technological advancement, and the difficulty of engineering workarounds.
  3. Market Relevance: Total addressable market for the underlying tech, density and capital resources of prospective licensees/acquirers, and verifiable Evidence of Use (EoU) against commercial infringers.

What Is the Fundamental Difference Between Patent Assignment and Licensing?

A real estate analogy provides immediate clarity:

Patent Assignment: Analogous to selling real estate. Full title and proprietary ownership are permanently transferred. Upon closing, the assignor retains zero residual rights in the patent.

Patent Licensing: Analogous to leasing property. Title remains vested in the patentee, who grants defined rights to exploit the invention within specified territories, terms, and fields of use.

What Are the Differences Between Exclusive, Sole, and Non-Exclusive Licenses?

These represent the three standard licensing modalities under Chinese patent practice, in descending order of exclusivity:

  • Exclusive License: The licensee holds sole exploitation rights to the exclusion of all others—even the patent owner is barred from practicing the invention.
  • Sole License: Both the patentee and the licensee may practice the invention, but the patentee is prohibited from granting licenses to any third parties.
  • Non-Exclusive License: The patentee may license the technology to multiple licensees while retaining the right to practice the invention.

Accordingly, licensing consideration (royalties) typically scales downward from exclusive to non-exclusive structures.

II. Procedures & Legal Practice

What Official Formalities Are Required for Patent Assignment?

Patent assignment is not complete upon the execution of a transfer contract. The critical statutory step is filing a joint Recordal of Bibliographic Change with the China National Intellectual Property Administration (CNIPA).

Title transfer takes legal effect only when CNIPA approves the change, issues an Official Notification of Completion, and registers the assignment in the Patent Gazette.

Is Patent License Contract Recordal Required with CNIPA?

While Chinese Patent Law does not make license recordal a prerequisite for contractual validity between the parties, we strongly advise completing official recordal for two critical reasons:

  1. Constructive Notice Against Bona Fide Third Parties: Recorded licenses remain enforceable against subsequent assignees or creditors who acquire the patent without notice.
  2. Regulatory & Tax Compliance: Essential proof for cross-border foreign exchange remittances, technology import filings, and tax deduction eligibility.

What Are the Standard Payment Structures for Patent Royalties?

Licensing payment terms can be structured flexibly based on business risk allocation:

  • Fixed Lump-Sum: A definitive fixed amount payable either upfront or in milestone tranches.
  • Running Royalty: A percentage calculated on net sales revenue or operating profit, frequently anchored by an annual minimum royalty guarantee.
  • Hybrid (Upfront Fee + Running Royalty): An initial execution fee paired with ongoing output-based royalties.

Beyond Royalty Rates, What Contractual Terms Are Critical in Negotiations?

In sophisticated patent transactions, valuation is only one factor. The following terms are decisive in mitigating post-closing exposure:

  • Field of Use & Territory: Explicit geographical boundaries, product categories, and duration.
  • Sublicensing Rights: Whether the licensee may grant sublicenses to subsidiaries or supply chain partners.
  • Grantbacks & Improvements: Ownership and cross-licensing of post-execution derivative technology and improvements.
  • Maintenance & Annuities: Defining which party is obligated to monitor and disburse ongoing patent office annuities.
  • Representations & Warranties: Title validity, lack of undisclosed encumbrances, and non-infringement covenants.
  • Confidentiality & Remedies: Liquidated damages, dispute resolution forums, and injunctive relief protocols.
