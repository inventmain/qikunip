I. Infringement & Jurisdiction

How Is Patent Infringement Legally Determined?

Determining patent infringement is a rigorous legal and technical analysis. The fundamental procedure involves mapping the accused product or process element-by-element against the patent's claims.

The two fundamental principles of infringement determination are:

  1. All-Elements Rule: If the accused product embodies every single technical feature recited in an independent claim, it falls squarely within the scope of protection, establishing literal infringement.
  2. Doctrine of Equivalents: Even if one or more features differ literally, infringement is established if the accused feature performs substantially the same function, in substantially the same way, to achieve substantially the same result, and is obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art without creative effort.

Where Are Patent Infringement Lawsuits Litigated in China?

Under PRC Civil Procedure and Patent Law, patent infringement actions fall under the jurisdiction of the Intermediate People's Courts or specialized Intellectual Property Courts located at the place of infringement or the defendant's domicile.

To ensure technical expertise and uniformity, China has established specialized Intellectual Property Courts in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and the Hainan Free Trade Port, complemented by the Intellectual Property Tribunal of the Supreme People's Court serving as a unified national appellate court for patent cases.

What Is the Step-by-Step Trial Workflow for Patent Enforcement?

A typical first-instance patent litigation proceeding encompasses:

Technical claim mapping & evidence preservation (notarial purchase) → Cease-and-desist letter (optional) → Pleadings drafting & formal docketing → Judicial service & defendant's written defense → Pre-trial evidence exchange & claim construction → Oral hearing (fact-finding & trial advocacy) → Judicial Judgment.

The first-instance trial timeline typically ranges from 6 to 12 months, with complex multi-jurisdictional disputes extending longer.

II. Damages & Defense Strategies

How Are Patent Infringement Damages Calculated Under Chinese Law?

People's Courts calculate patent infringement damage awards according to a strict statutory hierarchy:

  1. Actual Losses: Proven economic losses suffered by the patentee due to the infringement;
  2. Infringer's Unlawful Profits: Inability to prove actual loss shifts the calculation to profits derived by the infringer;
  3. Reasonable Royalty Multiplier: Benchmarked against established commercial license royalties for the patent;
  4. Statutory Damages: Where financial proof is absent, courts exercise judicial discretion to award between RMB 30,000 and RMB 5,000,000.
Crucially, for willful infringement with severe circumstances, Chinese courts may award punitive damages ranging from 1x to 5x the base calculated amount.

What Are the Most Common Defense Strategies Used by Accused Infringers?

Defendants in patent infringement litigations routinely deploy four primary defense doctrines:

  • Patent Invalidation Petition: The primary counter-strike. The defendant petitions CNIPA to invalidate the asserted patent, seeking to nullify the foundation of the civil infringement suit.
  • Non-Infringement Defense: Demonstrating that the accused technology omits at least one claim element, avoiding both literal and doctrine of equivalents coverage.
  • Prior Art Defense: Proving that the accused implementation was entirely disclosed in the prior art before the patent's filing or priority date.
  • Prior User Rights: Proving that the defendant had already manufactured identical products or made necessary preparations prior to the patent application date.

Pre-Suit Preliminary Injunctions

A pre-suit preliminary injunction (legally designated as "Pre-litigation Act Preservation") is a potent interim equitable remedy.

Where imminent infringement threatens irreparable harm (such as unauthorized exhibition or distribution at major trade expos), the patentee may petition the court for an emergency order commanding the infringer to immediately cease production, exhibition, or sales prior to full trial on the merits.

The evidentiary threshold for injunctive relief is exceptionally demanding, requiring indisputable claim overlap and substantial counter-security guarantees.
